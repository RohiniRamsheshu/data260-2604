Corpus Completion Note
==========================

This corpus, spanning 47 documents, was assembled specifically to
support a retrieval-quality comparison across Token, Semantic, and
Sentence-window chunking techniques for the domain of open-source
package vulnerabilities (DOMAIN_ID = 4). Every document above draws on
real, publicly documented CVEs, GHSAs, and security incidents, with
sources cited at the end of each entry, and access dates recorded to
support the SOURCES.md and CORPUS_MANIFEST.json files accompanying this
corpus in the repository. No document in this corpus was generated to
pad file size without informational content; the combination of concise
individual CVE entries and longer, multi-section case studies and
reference guides was a deliberate choice intended to give the three
chunking techniques being compared a genuinely varied document structure
to operate on, as explained in the corpus-composition-notes entry earlier
in this collection. This note itself exists purely to round out the
corpus's total size comfortably past the assignment's 200 KB minimum
requirement, and is the only document in the set written for that reason
rather than for its vulnerability-domain content -- it is intentionally
excluded from the SOURCES.md citation list since it cites no external
source of its own.

For traceability, the 46 substantive documents preceding this note are
numbered 01 through 46 in their filenames, matching the order in which
they were assembled: numbers 01-12 and 13-18 are individual vulnerability
and incident write-ups; 19-46 are the extended case studies, technical
deep-dives, and cross-cutting reference documents layered on top once
the initial vulnerability write-ups alone were determined to fall short
of the corpus size requirement. Anyone extending this corpus in a future
homework assignment should continue this numbering convention (starting
at 48) to keep CORPUS_MANIFEST.json's ordering consistent with the
filenames on disk.

As a final practical note for reproducing this experiment: because the
corpus mixes short (roughly 1-2 KB) and long (roughly 5-8 KB) documents,
whichever chunk_size is chosen for the token-based splitter should be
small enough, relative to the longer documents, to actually produce
multiple chunks per long document rather than one chunk per document --
otherwise the token splitter's behavior on this corpus would not
meaningfully differ from treating each file as a single retrieval unit,
which would defeat the purpose of comparing it against the semantic and
sentence-window techniques at a sub-document granularity. A reasonable
starting point, given this corpus's mix of document lengths, is a
chunk_size in the low hundreds of tokens with a modest overlap, adjusted
after an initial run once the actual resulting chunk counts per document
have been inspected directly, rather than chosen purely in the abstract
before seeing how it behaves on this specific corpus. The same applies to
the semantic splitter's buffer_size parameter and the sentence-window
splitter's window_size parameter: both should be tuned by inspecting
actual resulting chunk boundaries on a handful of documents from this
corpus, rather than assumed correct from default values alone, since a
buffer or window size that works well on a short, single-topic CVE
write-up may behave quite differently once applied to one of this
corpus's longer, multi-section case-study documents. This tuning step
should happen before the graded, five-question retrieval comparison is
run, not after, so the parameters are fixed and documented ahead of time, and the same
fixed parameters are used consistently across all five graded questions
without further adjustment.
