Notes on This Corpus's Composition, for the Chunking/Retrieval Experiment
==============================================================================

This final note documents the corpus's composition and intended use for
the retrieval-comparison experiment this corpus was assembled to support,
distinct from the technical vulnerability content itself covered in the
other 43 documents.

Composition summary
--------------------------
The corpus consists of 44 local text documents, falling into three broad
categories: (1) individual vulnerability write-ups for specific CVEs
across the npm, PyPI, and Maven ecosystems, spanning severities from
medium to critical and including both remote-code-execution and denial-
of-service impact types; (2) extended case studies and historical
incident analyses, covering both traditional code-level vulnerabilities
(Heartbleed, Shellshock, the Equifax/Struts2 breach) and supply-chain-
specific incidents (the XZ Utils backdoor, several npm maintainer-
account-compromise incidents, and two protestware incidents); and (3)
cross-cutting reference material explaining the surrounding
infrastructure and processes -- vulnerability identifier systems, CVSS
scoring, patch prioritization frameworks, secure coding guidance, and
session/cookie security -- that provide context spanning multiple
individual CVEs rather than describing any single vulnerability.

Why this mix supports a meaningful chunking comparison
-------------------------------------------------------------
A corpus consisting only of short, uniform, single-fact documents (as an
earlier, smaller draft of this corpus initially did) would not
sufficiently exercise the differences between token-based, semantic, and
sentence-window chunking, since with very short source documents, all
three techniques tend to converge toward producing similar, whole-
document-sized chunks. This final corpus intentionally combines short,
dense, single-topic documents (particularly the original 12 CVE write-
ups, before their later technical-detail expansions) with much longer,
multi-section documents covering several related sub-topics each (the
extended case studies and cross-cutting reference documents). This
variety in document length and internal topical structure is specifically
what should allow token-based chunking (blind to structure), semantic
chunking (sensitive to topic shifts, which the longer multi-section
documents actually contain, unlike the shorter single-topic CVE write-
ups), and sentence-window chunking (uniformly fine-grained regardless of
document length) to produce meaningfully different chunk sets when run
over this same underlying corpus, which is the specific comparison this
experiment is designed to surface.

A note on document overlap and cross-references
-------------------------------------------------------
Several documents in this corpus deliberately reference the same
underlying vulnerabilities from different angles -- for example, Log4Shell
appears in its own dedicated entry, in the Java-deserialization-history
overview, in the CWE-catalog entry, in the CVSS-worked-examples entry,
and in the dedicated organizational-response case study. This is an
intentional design choice, not redundant filler: it means a retrieval
query about Log4j could plausibly match chunks from several different
source documents, each emphasizing a different facet (technical
mechanism, weakness classification, severity scoring, or organizational
response) -- a realistic property of a real-world vulnerability corpus,
where the same vulnerability is genuinely discussed from multiple
angles across multiple sources, and a good retrieval system should
surface the most relevant facet for a given query rather than always
returning the same single "canonical" chunk regardless of what was
actually asked.
